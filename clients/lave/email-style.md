# How Dan writes email (Lave)

Read before drafting. Each lesson was learned by comparing a draft with what Dan sent, and approved by him. Rules only: no email content, names or client data.

## Standing rules

- Reply in the language of the email being answered; whole-team emails are bilingual (English, divider, Spanish).
- Dan organises information visually: tables for items with attributes, inline screenshots for anything visible on a screen.

## Lessons

<!-- YYYY-MM-DD — lesson -->
- 2026-10-01 — When an ADO card was created for the request, the reply includes a named link to the card before the sign-off.
- 2026-10-01 — When the answer to a request is "already done", reply with a one-line confirmation. No figures, tables or follow-up questions unless Dan asks for them.
- 2026-10-05 — Status or hand-off emails that list columns, jobs or items go out as HTML: one table per group (name | ID | counterpart | ID), named links instead of bare URLs, numbered bold section headers, one sentence of explanation per section. The spd-column-sync tool's plain-text draft is not enough; build the body with gmail-draft (plain + HTML).
- 2026-10-05 — Replies go to everyone on the thread (reply-all), not only the sender, and open with "Hi team" when there are several.
- 2026-10-05 — A link to a file uses the file's title as the link text, never a bare URL.
- 2026-10-05 — A list of items the reader must choose among goes one per line, not comma-separated inside a sentence.
- 2026-10-06 — When an email tells people where to send IT requests, give the helpdesk address and Dan's own address together.
